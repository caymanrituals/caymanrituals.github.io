Keep the island.

Cayman is not a beach. From the floor of the Cayman Trench to the top of the Bluff is almost eight vertical kilometres, and every road and house on these islands sits in the top fifty metres of it.

We make one thing from each layer. Four elements, and the fifth at the bottom of the sea.

It began at a kitchen table, with a saucepan and a coconut shell.

Before there was a brand, there was a ritual.

It was wax poured into shells cleaned by hand, for the people around the table. The island was in every one of them: the salt, the thatch, the rock.

Cayman Rituals is that habit, held to a higher standard. Made slowly, in small numbers, from the only place on earth these layers exist.

The pieces

Everything we make starts somewhere on this line, between the Bluff and the Trench. Nothing on it can be made anywhere else.

  • The KeeperA lid you buy once. It moves onto every candle refill after that. Turned from caymanite in numbered editions, as the stone allows, or poured in bands by hand, like the candle.
  • The SlabA slice of the stone, cut so its bands run level, set in a plain gold frame. A section you can wear, in numbered editions, as the stone allows.
  • The RopeSilver thatch rope was a Caymanian export within living memory. Ours is a bracelet, plaited by hand from the same palm, and every piece is numbered from No. 001.
  • Eau de parfumPlain heavy glass, with the cap wrapped by hand in the same plaited thatch.
  • The Section CandlePoured in three bands, like the stone. As it burns, it melts down through its own layers.

Take only what the island can spare.

Caymanite was laid down inside the Bluff over millions of years. It will not be dug out for us. Every material we use has a rule like that.

Caymanite
Only from documented sources, recorded piece by piece, and kept for numbered editions. The everyday Keeper is poured in bands instead.
Silver thatch
Fronds cut by hand, the old way. Never the palm.
Coconut
Fifty shells a year, cleaned and prepared by hand. No more.
Glass
Plain and heavy. The Keeper, bought once, moves onto every refill.
Brass
When the Rope's cord wears, it is replaced. The ends are kept.
Never
Black coral. Some of the island belongs to the sea.

Every numbered piece goes into the register: what it is made of, where that came from, and how it was made. The register opens with Rope No. 001.

Made here. Nowhere else.

First Pour

Fifty candles, poured into coconut shells, once a year.

The first Cayman Rituals candles were poured into coconut shells at a kitchen table. Every year Bianca pours fifty more the same way, cleaned and prepared by hand, each one numbered and dated. When they are gone, that year is gone.
